01 / The experience

A familiar world, seen in a different light.

Zepholume brings light, sky and atmosphere together while keeping Minecraft's blocks, colours and landscapes recognisable.

Light & colour

Sun and moon response, block-light warmth and controlled colour treatment give scenes a more considered sense of depth.

Sky & atmosphere

Sky, fog, clouds and weather shape the mood as time and conditions change. The Nether and End retain their own atmospheric paths.

Water & surroundings

Analytical water shading adds surface response; Balanced and higher profiles also enable gentle water movement. Effects vary by profile.

02 / Find your balance

Five profiles. One philosophy.

Start with Balanced, the source default and recommended starting point. Choose a lower tier to reduce optional work or a higher tier for more analytical detail.

Potato — The minimum-work tier retains the basic scene grade and loader sky while leaving major analytical effects and water waves off.

Low — Adds modest lighting, cloud, weather and water response, without water vertex movement.

Balanced • Recommended
Adds material response and bounded water movement for the intended everyday experience.

High — Raises selected lighting, cloud, water and weather quality.

Ultra — Uses the highest configured cloud, water and weather tiers within the same renderer.

The profiles are defined in the pack; their menu behaviour in the final V1.0.6 artifact has not been fully qualified in Minecraft. Existing settings can override defaults, so select a profile explicitly when starting.

03 / Designed with restraint

Atmosphere without an oversized pipeline.

Zepholume uses a direct rendering path and adjusts analytical detail across its profiles. It does not add shadow maps, screen-space reflections or a bloom pipeline. The aim is to make a considered visual difference while keeping rendering work bounded.

That architecture is a design choice, not a promise of higher FPS. Performance depends on your GPU, resolution, render distance, loader, mods and scene.

04 / Get started

From download to daylight.

  1. Set up a Minecraft Java instance with an appropriate Iris shader loader for your game version.
  2. Download Zepholume from the CurseForge Files page.
  3. Place the intact ZIP in that instance's shaderpacks folder. Do not extract it.
  4. Open the shader-pack menu in Minecraft, select Zepholume and choose Balanced to start.

Compatibility: Iris is the documented primary target; Oculus is an intended compatibility path that has not been runtime-qualified for the final V1.0.6 artifact. OptiFine is unsupported. CurseForge version tags describe file listings, not proof of gameplay testing for every version, loader or GPU.

05 / Need a hand?

Make the next step a clear one.

If performance is uncomfortable, try a lower profile and compare the same scene with the same settings. If the pack fails to load or looks wrong, check the instance, ZIP and loader versions, then try a minimal setup without unrelated rendering mods or resource packs.

For a useful report, include the Zepholume version and profile, Minecraft and loader versions, GPU and driver, reproduction steps, the complete latest.log, and a screenshot for visual issues.
