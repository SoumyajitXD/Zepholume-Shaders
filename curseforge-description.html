Why Zepholume?
Better atmosphere without forgetting the FPS counter.

Zepholume is built around a deliberately simple idea: Minecraft does not need a gigantic cinematic rendering pipeline to look substantially better.

The project spends its rendering budget on controlled lighting, environmental colour, sky and horizon treatment, fog, clouds, weather, water and scene depth while keeping the rendering route deliberately bounded.

The goal is not to win a checklist competition against heavyweight shaders. The goal is to make normal Minecraft exploration, building and survival feel richer while respecting frame time, portability and maintainability.

Improve the scene, not the renderer's résumé.
Zepholume prefers techniques with a clear visual payoff over expensive systems added merely because a feature list looks lonely without them.
 
V1.0.3
More consistent light. Cleaner maths. Stronger foundations.

V1.0.3 is a correctness, refinement and hardening release. Rather than bolting on another expensive rendering system, this update concentrates on making Zepholume's existing lighting and water paths behave more consistently while strengthening the validation used to keep subtle regressions out of releases.

  • Skylight transitions were corrected and hardened. The affected transition now uses the intended 0.04–0.65 range with mathematically equivalent Hermite-style interpolation that is explicitly regression-tested.
  • Block-light transitions received the same treatment. Warm artificial lighting now follows the corrected 0.10–0.90 transition range with stronger numerical regression coverage.
  • Interior and cave lighting remains better grounded. Celestial response continues to depend on skylight rather than pretending every underground block has direct access to the sky.
  • Block-light warmth stays contextual. Artificial warmth is driven by block-light dominance relative to skylight instead of indiscriminately tinting strongly sky-lit surfaces orange.
  • Water maths was cleaned up. An unnecessary encode/decode route was removed from the water path, eliminating needless precision churn while preserving the lightweight analytical design.
  • Water keeps its bounded grazing response. Zepholume retains its tuned Fresnel-inspired treatment and analytical celestial specular response without adding SSR, depth reconstruction or another framebuffer chain.
  • Shader maths received additional scrutiny. V1.0.3 focuses on equivalent, predictable expressions rather than clever-looking transformations that are difficult to verify.
  • Regression tests now inspect the actual shader source. Lighting tests derive constants from the GLSL implementation instead of quietly validating a separate mathematical model that could drift away from production code.
  • Boundary behaviour is tested explicitly. Numerical checks cover values below, at, inside, around the midpoint and above the affected transition regions.
  • Portability and release validation were strengthened. The existing profile, dimension and GLSL validation pipeline continues to guard against structural, compilation and shader-architecture regressions.
Tiny errors, measured instead of hand-waved
The V1.0.3 mathematical regression suite observed maximum errors of roughly 2.3×10-9 for the tested skylight path and 1.1×10-16 for the tested block-light path. The removed water float32 encode/decode route showed a round-trip error of roughly 1.19×10-7.

Those numbers are numerical-regression evidence, not an FPS benchmark and not proof that every GPU driver renders identically. A calculator is useful; it still has not secretly become a graphics card.

 
Visual Direction
Atmosphere that behaves like one system.
Day, night & twilight

Terrain, sky, clouds and fog share continuous environmental behaviour so sunrise, sunset, daylight and night transition as parts of the same world rather than unrelated colour presets.

Sun, moon & directional light

Zepholume uses separate loader-provided sun and moon directions for bounded celestial lighting. Skylight grounding prevents that directional response from behaving as though caves have developed transparent ceilings.

Sky & horizon

The analytical sky uses controlled elevation and horizon behaviour, loader-provided scene colour, restrained twilight warmth and celestial glow. Horizon treatment is coordinated with fog to reduce visible atmospheric seams.

Fog & dimensional atmosphere

Fog is treated as part of the scene. The Overworld gains atmospheric depth, underwater views gain restrained tinting on supported profiles, and the Nether and End use independent ambient treatment rather than carrying unnecessary Overworld celestial maths.

Block lighting

Interior warmth is driven by the relationship between block light and skylight. V1.0.3 further hardens the transition maths so artificial lighting can feel richer indoors without forcing an orange cast over strongly sky-lit surfaces.

Weather

Rain and storms soften directional lighting and adjust atmospheric colour without requiring a separate heavyweight weather renderer.

Water

Water combines a bounded sky/transmission response with tuned grazing reflectance. Balanced and higher profiles add low-amplitude frame-time-driven vertex movement, while higher tiers refine analytical water quality without introducing screen-space reflections, depth reconstruction or additional framebuffer passes. V1.0.3 also removes unnecessary encode/decode work from the water path.

Colour & scene depth

Exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature, material orientation and highlight compression are used conservatively to give Minecraft stronger depth without burying its recognisable art direction under aggressive grading.

 
Quality Profiles
Five profiles. Real capability boundaries.

The selected profile establishes a hard feature baseline. Lower overrides can reduce work, but they cannot quietly enable effects above the chosen tier. In other words, Potato is actually Potato—not Ultra wearing a fake moustache.

Potato
The compatibility and minimum-work tier. Potato keeps direct scene grading and loader sky/fog while compiling out analytical face, material, cloud, water, weather and underwater-colour work.
Low
The lightweight target. Low enables restrained directional face/cloud response, basic analytical water and weather treatment, plus subtle underwater fog tinting while keeping the renderer tightly bounded.
Balanced Default & recommended starting point
The intended everyday Zepholume experience. Balanced adds stronger material response, atmospheric depth, continuous time transitions and animated low-amplitude two-wave water without changing the underlying direct-path architecture.
High
Raises bounded face, cloud, water and weather detail for systems with more headroom. It remains inside the same one-colour-target architecture rather than opening a hidden post-processing pipeline.
Ultra
The highest analytical Zepholume tier. Ultra maximises the current direct-path effects without adding shadows, SSR, temporal history, volumetrics, bloom or extra colour buffers.

Ultra Lite remains available as a deprecated compatibility alias for Low. And if Balanced looks excellent while running better, use Balanced. Selecting Ultra is still not a moral achievement.

Performance Philosophy
Performance is part of the visual experience.

A beautiful still image means very little if playing the game feels awful.

Zepholume therefore treats shader cost, frame-time behaviour, stability, portability and maintainability as design constraints from the beginning. Lower profiles compile out optional work while higher tiers increase bounded analytical quality instead of activating an entirely different renderer.

Direct-path by design
The pack contains 24 GLSL vertex/fragment program pairs and writes to one colour target. High and Ultra increase analytical detail inside that architecture rather than adding framebuffer choreography behind the user's back.

V1.0.3 does not claim a specific FPS uplift. Static shader complexity, source size, mathematical regression tests and successful standalone compilation are useful engineering evidence; none of them magically become a controlled gameplay benchmark when nobody is looking.

What Zepholume deliberately does not do

The current architecture intentionally avoids heavyweight systems whose cost or complexity does not fit the project's goals:

  • no shadow-map pipeline;
  • no screen-space reflections;
  • no SSAO pipeline;
  • no bloom pipeline;
  • no temporal-history or TAA system;
  • no volumetric-lighting pipeline;
  • no ray tracing;
  • no compute-shader passes;
  • no geometry or tessellation shader stages;
  • no SSBO/image pipeline;
  • no collection of unnecessary full-resolution intermediate colour buffers;
  • no heavyweight post-processing chain.

These are intentional architectural boundaries. Zepholume aims for strong visual return from a restrained renderer instead of treating GPU time like an unlimited credit card.

Architectural efficiency is not the same thing as measured FPS. Real performance still depends on GPU, resolution, render distance, driver, loader, mods, resource packs and the scene being rendered.

Validation
Test what can be tested. Label the rest honestly.

V1.0.3 expands Zepholume's static and numerical validation rather than treating “it compiled once” as a release methodology. The project evaluates generated shader variants, validates the declared profile/dimension matrix, compiles expanded GLSL with standalone glslangValidator and now includes source-derived mathematical regression coverage for critical lighting transitions.

  • 48 shader stages are structurally checked;
  • 210 unique expanded GLSL stages are covered by standalone compilation;
  • five profiles plus the deprecated Ultra Lite alias are included in generated validation;
  • Overworld, Nether and End wrappers are covered;
  • two loader macro models are exercised;
  • regressions can reject accidental composite, deferred or shadow program families;
  • Potato compile-outs and Nether/End celestial isolation receive explicit evaluated-source checks;
  • lighting regression tests parse the actual GLSL constants instead of duplicating them independently;
  • skylight and block-light transition boundaries, interiors and midpoint behaviour receive explicit numerical checks;
  • removed water precision work is covered by regression evidence.
Important: standalone GLSL compilation, mathematical regression tests and mocked portability checks do not prove that an Iris/Oculus-patched shader rendered correctly on a particular real GPU or achieved any particular FPS.
 
Compatibility & Runtime Status
Know exactly what has—and has not—been verified.

Zepholume V1.0.3 is developed for the modern Iris and Oculus shader ecosystems. The project's prepared validation baseline uses Minecraft Java Edition 1.20.1 with Iris/Fabric/Sodium and Oculus/Forge/Embeddium environments. Zepholume does not contain its own shader loader.

  • Primary validation target: Minecraft Java Edition 1.20.1.
  • Iris environment: Iris 1.7.6 + Sodium 0.5.13 on Minecraft 1.20.1.
  • Oculus environment: Oculus 1.8.0 + Embeddium 0.3.31 + Forge 47.4.22 on Minecraft 1.20.1.
  • Java: Java 17 in the prepared 1.20.1 validation environments.
  • OptiFine: not supported or tested for this release.
V1.0.3 still has runtime qualification limits.
Source validation, mathematical regression tests, GLSL compilation, portability checks and release hardening provide useful evidence, but controlled real-world visual QA and A/B gameplay benchmarking remain separate requirements. Do not read static validation as proof of a particular FPS uplift or perfect rendering on every GPU/driver combination.

That distinction matters. Zepholume is intentionally conservative about compatibility and performance claims rather than calling a source compiler a graphics card and hoping nobody notices.

Project-wide CurseForge version metadata can cover more versions than the evidence for one particular release. For V1.0.3, rely on the release file information and maintained compatibility documentation when determining what has actually been validated.

Installation
From ZIP to shader menu.
  1. Use a supported Minecraft instance with a compatible Iris or Oculus shader setup.
  2. Download Zepholume-Shaders-1.0.3.zip.
  3. Place the ZIP directly inside that Minecraft instance's shaderpacks directory.
  4. Launch Minecraft and open the shader-pack menu.
  5. Select Zepholume Shaders.
  6. Start with Balanced. Move to Low/Potato when reducing GPU cost, or High/Ultra when you have comfortable headroom.

Do not extract the release ZIP. For compatibility testing, a disposable or isolated Minecraft instance is strongly preferable to turning your 300-mod survival installation into a laboratory experiment.

Having problems?
Debug the stack, not random checkboxes.

For low FPS or frame-time trouble, move down one Zepholume profile first and compare the same scene using the same graphics settings. Also check resolution, render distance, simulation distance, resource packs and other rendering-heavy mods.

For shader loading, compilation or visual corruption:

  • verify that you are actually testing Zepholume-Shaders-1.0.3.zip;
  • verify your exact Minecraft and Iris/Oculus versions;
  • check Sodium/Embeddium and Fabric/Forge versions where applicable;
  • confirm the ZIP is inside the correct instance's shaderpacks directory;
  • check the GPU driver if the problem appeared after a driver update;
  • reproduce the problem in the smallest practical instance without unrelated rendering mods or resource packs;
  • test shader reloads and dimension transitions when reporting intermittent problems.
Useful bug-report evidence

Minecraft version • Iris/Oculus version • Fabric/Forge version • Sodium/Embeddium version • Zepholume version and profile • GPU • driver version • Java version • complete latest.log • relevant rendering/resource-pack mods • screenshot or short video for visual problems • whether the issue reproduces in a minimal instance.
