Why Zepholume?
Better atmosphere without forgetting the FPS counter.

Zepholume is built around a deliberately simple idea: Minecraft does not need a gigantic cinematic rendering pipeline to look substantially better.

The project spends its rendering budget on controlled lighting, environmental colour, sky and horizon treatment, fog, clouds, weather, water and scene depth while keeping the rendering route deliberately bounded.

The goal is not to win a checklist competition against heavyweight shaders. The goal is to make normal Minecraft exploration, building and survival feel richer while respecting frame time, portability and maintainability.

Improve the scene, not the renderer's résumé.
Zepholume prefers techniques with a clear visual payoff over expensive systems added merely because a feature list looks lonely without them.
 
V1.0.4
Less wasted water work. Same restrained renderer.

V1.0.4 is a focused optimisation and maintenance release. It does not add another rendering system, rewrite the visual identity or invent an FPS percentage from thin air. The production change targets unnecessary celestial-specular work in the existing water shader while preserving Zepholume's direct-path architecture.

  • Water celestial-specular work now has exact-zero gating. When an existing sun or moon contribution is mathematically zero, Zepholume can skip the lobe-only calculations that would otherwise produce a value multiplied by zero anyway.
  • Full daylight can avoid unnecessary moon-specular work. When the existing uniform contribution makes the moon lobe exactly inactive, its direction-normalisation and related lobe calculations do not need to run.
  • Equivalent non-zero lighting paths are preserved. Twilight and other states where a celestial contribution is active keep the existing calculation order instead of replacing the water model with a cheaper approximation.
  • The gate is based on draw-uniform state, not a noisy per-pixel surface branch. It does not introduce a fragment-varying normal-dot decision merely to manufacture an optimisation.
  • No new shader interface was required. The production change adds no new varying, uniform, colour target, vendor extension or GPU-specific rendering path.
  • The direct GLSL 330 compatibility architecture remains intact. V1.0.4 still uses the same deliberately bounded forward rendering design.
  • The fog endpoint-hoisting experiment was not promoted. It remains preserved as a separate deferred experiment because static equivalence alone was not enough evidence to justify shipping it as a performance optimisation.
  • Packaging and validation were hardened. The final release is produced from a controlled source allow-list with deterministic packaging behaviour rather than scooping development debris into the public ZIP.
  • No measured FPS claim is attached to V1.0.4. The retained change removes provably unnecessary work in exact-zero states, but real frame-time impact still requires controlled runtime measurement.
Optimisation without performance fan-fiction
V1.0.4 has mathematical and static evidence that the gated zero-contribution calculations cannot affect their final zero-valued result. That establishes correctness for the tested predicate cases. It does not establish a universal FPS increase, because a shader compiler, driver and GPU remain inconveniently more complicated than a source-code diff.

The fog-hoist treatment was deliberately left out of production. Keeping an optimisation because it looks clever would be benchmark cosplay; V1.0.4 ships the smaller, lower-risk change instead.

 
Visual Direction
Atmosphere that behaves like one system.
Day, night & twilight

Terrain, sky, clouds and fog share continuous environmental behaviour so sunrise, sunset, daylight and night transition as parts of the same world rather than unrelated colour presets.

Sun, moon & directional light

Zepholume uses separate loader-provided sun and moon directions for bounded celestial lighting. Skylight grounding prevents that directional response from behaving as though caves have developed transparent ceilings.

Sky & horizon

The analytical sky uses controlled elevation and horizon behaviour, loader-provided scene colour, restrained twilight warmth and celestial glow. Horizon treatment is coordinated with fog to reduce visible atmospheric seams.

Fog & dimensional atmosphere

Fog is treated as part of the scene. The Overworld gains atmospheric depth, underwater views gain restrained tinting on supported profiles, and the Nether and End use independent ambient treatment rather than carrying unnecessary Overworld celestial maths.

Block lighting

Interior warmth is driven by the relationship between block light and skylight. Artificial lighting gains richer warmth where block light actually dominates instead of indiscriminately painting strongly sky-lit surfaces orange.

Weather

Rain and storms soften directional lighting and adjust atmospheric colour without requiring a separate heavyweight weather renderer.

Water

Water combines bounded sky/transmission response with tuned grazing reflectance. Low and Balanced retain the lighter fourth-power artistic response; High and Ultra use a refined fifth-power Fresnel-Schlick-shaped curve. Balanced and higher profiles add bounded vertex-driven water movement. V1.0.4 additionally skips celestial-specular lobe work when the existing contribution is exactly zero, without introducing SSR, depth reconstruction or another framebuffer pass.

Colour & scene depth

Exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature, material orientation and highlight compression are used conservatively to give Minecraft stronger depth without burying its recognisable art direction under aggressive grading.

 
Quality Profiles
Five profiles. Real capability boundaries.

The selected profile establishes a hard feature baseline. Lower overrides can reduce work, but they cannot quietly enable effects above the chosen tier. In other words, Potato is actually Potato—not Ultra wearing a fake moustache.

Potato
The compatibility and minimum-work tier. Potato keeps the direct scene grade and loader sky/fog behaviour while compiling out analytical face, material, cloud, water, weather and analytical-sky work. Underwater fog colour remains loader-provided instead of enabling Zepholume's additional tint.
Low
The lightweight target. Low enables restrained directional face/cloud response, basic analytical water, weather treatment and subtle underwater fog tinting while keeping water vertex movement disabled.
Balanced Default & recommended starting point
The intended everyday Zepholume experience. Balanced adds material response, atmospheric depth, continuous time transitions and animated low-amplitude two-wave water without changing the underlying direct-path architecture.
High
Raises bounded face, cloud, water and weather detail for systems with more headroom. Water also moves to the refined fifth-power grazing-response curve while staying inside the same one-colour-target renderer.
Ultra
The highest analytical Zepholume tier. Ultra maximises the current bounded direct-path effects without adding shadows, SSR, temporal history, volumetrics, bloom or extra colour buffers.

Ultra Lite remains available as a deprecated compatibility alias for Low. And if Balanced looks excellent while running better, use Balanced. Selecting Ultra is still not a moral achievement.

Performance Philosophy
Performance is part of the visual experience.

A beautiful still image means very little if playing the game feels awful.

Zepholume therefore treats shader cost, frame-time behaviour, stability, portability and maintainability as design constraints from the beginning. Lower profiles compile out optional work while higher tiers increase bounded analytical quality instead of activating an entirely different renderer.

Direct-path by design
The pack contains 24 GLSL vertex/fragment program pairs and writes to one colour target. High and Ultra increase analytical detail inside that architecture rather than adding framebuffer choreography behind the user's back.

V1.0.4 intentionally makes no specific FPS or frame-time uplift claim. Exact-zero gating removes unnecessary source-level work in qualifying water states, but static source analysis and mathematical equivalence do not magically become controlled GPU benchmarks when nobody is looking.

What Zepholume deliberately does not do

The current architecture intentionally avoids heavyweight systems whose cost or complexity does not fit the project's goals:

  • no shadow-map pipeline;
  • no screen-space reflections;
  • no SSAO pipeline;
  • no bloom pipeline;
  • no temporal-history or TAA system;
  • no volumetric-lighting pipeline;
  • no ray tracing;
  • no compute-shader passes;
  • no geometry or tessellation shader stages;
  • no SSBO/image pipeline;
  • no collection of unnecessary full-resolution intermediate colour buffers;
  • no heavyweight post-processing chain.

These are intentional architectural boundaries. Zepholume aims for strong visual return from a restrained renderer instead of treating GPU time like an unlimited credit card.

Architectural efficiency is not the same thing as measured FPS. Real performance still depends on GPU, resolution, render distance, driver, loader, mods, resource packs and the scene being rendered.

Validation
Test what can be tested. Label the rest honestly.

V1.0.4 retains Zepholume's structural, mathematical, portability and standalone GLSL validation rather than treating “it compiled once” as a release methodology. The final production change also has dedicated source-locked coverage for its exact-zero water-specular predicates.

  • 48 authored shader stages are structurally checked;
  • generated profile, dimension and loader-macro variants are evaluated before standalone GLSL compilation;
  • five profiles plus the deprecated Ultra Lite alias remain covered by generated validation;
  • Overworld, Nether and End wrappers are checked;
  • two loader macro models are exercised by static generation;
  • regressions reject accidental composite, deferred and shadow program families;
  • Potato analytical-work compile-outs and dimensional celestial isolation receive evaluated-source checks;
  • lighting regression tests continue to inspect the actual GLSL constants;
  • V1.0.4 adds dedicated float32 predicate coverage for the retained water celestial-specular gate;
  • the tested gate cases preserve an exact zero result whenever the skipped lobe contribution is exactly zero;
  • the maintained portability matrix exercises hundreds of mocked source/configuration cases, but remains a static model rather than a fleet of real GPUs;
  • release packaging is constrained to intentional public files rather than development artifacts and machine-local debris.
Important: standalone GLSL compilation, mathematical tests and mocked portability checks do not prove that an Iris/Oculus-patched shader rendered correctly on a particular GPU, achieved any specific FPS, or behaves identically across vendors and drivers.
 
Compatibility & Runtime Status
Separate static evidence from actual runtime evidence.

Zepholume V1.0.4 is maintained around the Iris/Oculus-style shader ecosystem, but the project deliberately distinguishes staged or static validation from actual game launch, shader activation, rendered-world qualification and visual QA.

  • Minecraft 1.20.1 • Fabric: Iris 1.7.6 + Sodium 0.5.13 are staged locally. Static validation passes; game launch, shader activation, world rendering and visual QA are not yet claimed.
  • Minecraft 1.20.1 • Forge: Oculus 1.8.0 + Embeddium 0.3.31 are staged locally. Static validation passes; game launch, shader activation, world rendering and visual QA are not yet claimed.
  • Minecraft 26.2 • Fabric: Iris 1.11.2 is a maintained static-validation target. Runtime qualification has not been completed.
  • Minecraft 26.2 • NeoForge: Iris 1.11.2 is a maintained static-validation target. Runtime qualification has not been completed.
  • Minecraft 26.2 • Forge: the Oculus Community Port 0.3.0-beta.1 path is experimental and its declared partial program support does not currently cover Zepholume's ordinary world gbuffers_* route sufficiently for a normal compatibility claim.
  • Java: Java 17 is used by the staged Minecraft 1.20.1 validation environments.
  • OptiFine: not supported or tested for V1.0.4.
V1.0.4 still has runtime qualification limits.
Static validation, mathematical regression tests, standalone GLSL compilation, portability modelling and release hardening establish source/package readiness. They do not substitute for loader-patched compilation, actual world rendering, visual comparison or controlled V1.0.3-versus-V1.0.4 frame-time capture.

That distinction matters. Zepholume would rather label evidence correctly than promote “the script was green” into “every GPU on Earth loves it.”

CurseForge project-wide game-version metadata can be broader than the evidence attached to one exact Zepholume release. Use the maintained compatibility documentation when deciding what has actually been qualified.

Installation
From ZIP to shader menu.
  1. Use a compatible Minecraft instance with an appropriate Iris or Oculus-style shader setup.
  2. Download Zepholume-Shaders-1.0.4.zip.
  3. Place the ZIP directly inside that Minecraft instance's shaderpacks directory.
  4. Launch Minecraft and open the shader-pack menu.
  5. Select Zepholume Shaders.
  6. Start with Balanced. Move to Low/Potato when reducing GPU cost, or High/Ultra when you have comfortable headroom.

Do not extract the release ZIP. For compatibility testing, a disposable or isolated Minecraft instance is strongly preferable to turning your 300-mod survival installation into a laboratory experiment.

Having problems?
Debug the stack, not random checkboxes.

For low FPS or frame-time trouble, move down one Zepholume profile first and compare the same scene using the same graphics settings. Also check resolution, render distance, simulation distance, resource packs and other rendering-heavy mods.

For shader loading, compilation or visual corruption:

  • verify that you are actually testing Zepholume-Shaders-1.0.4.zip;
  • verify your exact Minecraft and Iris/Oculus versions;
  • check Sodium/Embeddium and Fabric/Forge/NeoForge versions where applicable;
  • confirm the ZIP is inside the correct instance's shaderpacks directory;
  • check the GPU driver if the problem appeared after a driver update;
  • reproduce the problem in the smallest practical instance without unrelated rendering mods or resource packs;
  • test shader reloads and dimension transitions when reporting intermittent problems;
  • when testing a newer Minecraft version, distinguish a source/static-validation target from a version that has actually completed runtime qualification.
Useful bug-report evidence

Minecraft version • Iris/Oculus version • Fabric/Forge/NeoForge version • Sodium/Embeddium version • Zepholume version and profile • GPU • driver version • graphics API • Java version • complete latest.log • relevant rendering/resource-pack mods • screenshot or short video for visual problems • whether the issue reproduces in a minimal instance.
