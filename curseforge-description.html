Why Zepholume?
Better atmosphere without forgetting the FPS counter.

Zepholume is built around a deliberately unfashionable idea: a shader does not need an enormous renderer behind it to make Minecraft look substantially better.

Instead of treating every expensive rendering technique as a mandatory checkbox, the project concentrates its GPU budget on analytical lighting, environmental colour, sky and horizon treatment, fog, clouds, weather, water and scene depth.

The result is a shader whose visual direction and performance philosophy are designed together. Pretty screenshots matter. So does moving the camera without turning Minecraft into a PowerPoint presentation.

Improve the scene, not the renderer's résumé.
Zepholume prefers bounded visual techniques with a useful payoff instead of architecture cosplay and expensive effects added merely to inflate a feature list.
 
Visual Direction
Atmosphere that works as a system.
Day, night & twilight

Terrain, sky, clouds and fog use a more continuous environmental response so sunrise, sunset, daylight and night feel connected instead of behaving like unrelated colour presets.

Sky & horizon treatment

Analytical sky and horizon behaviour adds controlled colour separation, restrained twilight warmth and sun/moon glow while still respecting Minecraft and loader-provided scene information.

Fog & dimensional atmosphere

Fog is treated as part of the scene rather than an afterthought. The Overworld gains clearer atmospheric depth, while the Nether and End retain distinct, restrained treatment appropriate to each dimension.

Weather response

Rain can reduce saturation and alter the environmental presentation without replacing the entire renderer with a separate weather-effects pipeline.

Water

Water receives bounded transmission, sky-response and Fresnel-style treatment. From Balanced upward, subtle time-driven vertex waves give water real movement without SSR, depth reconstruction, simulation or another framebuffer hierarchy.

Colour, lighting & scene depth

Controlled exposure, contrast, saturation, temperature and material response are used to give Minecraft stronger depth and visual cohesion without burying its recognisable art direction beneath aggressive grading.

 
Quality Profiles
Five profiles. Actual differences in shader work.

Zepholume profiles are intended to change what the shader is allowed to do, not just adjust a few cosmetic constants while executing essentially the same workload underneath.

Potato
For very weak or integrated GPUs and unusually demanding modpacks. Potato removes or minimises optional work and compiles out additional analytical sky/cloud calculations. This is the lowest-cost Zepholume path.
Low
A lightweight environmental tier for lower-end systems. It enables restrained fog, atmosphere, cloud and water treatment while keeping material and higher-tier effects limited. Its water tier remains below the animated-wave path.
Balanced Recommended starting point
The intended middle ground for normal play. Balanced raises material and environmental treatment and enables subtle time-driven water movement without leaving Zepholume's lightweight architectural boundary.
High
For capable gaming GPUs with comfortable headroom on Balanced. High increases bounded analytical quality across areas such as face lighting, clouds, water and weather.
Ultra
The highest Zepholume quality tier. Ultra raises the project's bounded visual settings as far as the current renderer allows without quietly replacing the shader with a fundamentally heavier architecture.

If Balanced already looks excellent and runs better, use Balanced. Selecting Ultra is not a moral achievement.

V1.0.1
More atmosphere. Smarter rendering.

V1.0.1 is a refinement release: it improves Zepholume's existing lightweight foundation instead of bolting a collection of expensive new pipelines onto it.

  • More continuous sunlight and twilight response across terrain, sky, clouds and fog.
  • Refined sky, horizon and celestial glow with restrained twilight warmth and improved rainy-weather response.
  • Improved Overworld, Nether and End fog treatment while preserving each dimension's identity.
  • Time-driven water movement on Balanced and higher profiles without depth reconstruction, SSR or additional buffers.
  • Stronger compile-time scaling, including analytical sky/cloud work removed from Potato.
  • Defensive shader maths and redundant-work cleanup intended to reduce invalid rendering values and improve portability.

No specific FPS uplift is claimed for V1.0.1 without controlled runtime benchmarking. Static shader complexity is not a benchmark wearing a fake moustache.

Performance Philosophy
Performance is part of the visual experience.

A beautiful still image means very little if actually playing the game feels terrible.

Zepholume therefore treats rendering cost, frame-time behaviour, stability, portability and maintainability as design constraints from the beginning. Lower profiles can eliminate optional shader work at compile time, while higher profiles increase bounded analytical quality without opening an entirely different renderer.

Shared GLSL libraries also keep common lighting, atmosphere, colour, fog, weather and profile logic in consistent locations. That matters to players because fixes and tuning are less likely to drift between dozens of nearly identical shader stages.

What Zepholume deliberately does not do

The current architecture intentionally avoids heavyweight systems whose cost or complexity does not fit the project's goals:

  • no shadow-map pipeline;
  • no screen-space reflections;
  • no SSAO pipeline;
  • no temporal-history or TAA system;
  • no volumetric-lighting pipeline;
  • no ray tracing;
  • no compute-shader passes;
  • no geometry or tessellation shader stages;
  • no unnecessary collection of full-resolution intermediate colour buffers;
  • no heavyweight post-processing chain.

Those are architectural boundaries, not features that mysteriously went missing. Zepholume aims to get strong visual return from a restrained direct-path renderer instead of treating GPU time like an unlimited credit card.

This describes architectural efficiency, not measured FPS. Real performance still depends on GPU, resolution, render distance, Minecraft version, driver, loader, mods, resource packs and the scene being rendered.

 
Compatibility
Know the stack before you download.

Zepholume is a shader pack for Minecraft Java Edition. It does not contain its own shader loader.

  • Iris Shaders: supported shader-loader target in the Fabric ecosystem.
  • Oculus: supported shader-loader target in the Forge ecosystem.
  • OptiFine: not a supported Zepholume target. Use Iris or Oculus.
  • Minecraft 1.20+: tested support range.
  • Older than Minecraft 1.20: may work, but is not tested or guaranteed.

Zepholume is written around broadly portable GLSL instead of vendor-specific extensions and deliberately avoids requiring compute shaders, geometry/tessellation stages, ray-tracing hardware or similar heavyweight features.

That is a portability strategy, not a promise that every NVIDIA, AMD or Intel GPU, every driver release, every loader build and every mod combination has been exhaustively tested.

Important: check the game versions and loader requirements attached to the specific file you download. CurseForge dependency metadata and actual runtime shader-loader requirements are not necessarily the same thing.

Installation
From ZIP to shader menu.
  1. Install a compatible Iris or Oculus setup for your Minecraft version.
  2. Download the appropriate Zepholume release.
  3. Place the Zepholume .zip directly inside that Minecraft instance's shaderpacks directory.
  4. Launch Minecraft and open the shader-pack menu.
  5. Select Zepholume Shaders.
  6. Start with Balanced. Move down to Low/Potato if necessary, or upward to High/Ultra when you have comfortable GPU headroom.

Do not extract the release archive unless a specific release explicitly tells you to.

Having problems?
Debug the stack, not random checkboxes.

For low FPS or frame-time trouble, first move down one Zepholume profile and compare the same scene with the same graphics settings. Also check render distance, simulation distance, resolution, resource packs and other rendering-heavy mods.

For loading, compilation or visual-corruption problems:

  • verify your exact Minecraft and Iris/Oculus versions;
  • check Fabric/Forge and Sodium/Embeddium versions where applicable;
  • make sure Zepholume is inside the correct instance's shaderpacks directory;
  • check your GPU driver, especially if the problem appeared after a driver update;
  • test Zepholume in the smallest practical Minecraft instance without unrelated rendering/resource-pack mods;
  • reintroduce suspect mods gradually if the clean instance works.
When reporting a bug, include:

Minecraft version • Iris/Oculus version • Fabric/Forge version • Sodium/Embeddium version where relevant • Zepholume version and profile • GPU • GPU driver • Java version • complete latest.log • relevant resource/rendering mods • screenshot or short video for visual problems • whether the issue reproduces in a minimal instance.
